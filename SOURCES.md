# Sources: objective Vísindavefurinn articles

Articles on [Vísindavefurinn](https://www.visindavefur.is), the University of Iceland's
science web, that treat Marxism, socialism and the labour movement fairly. Use them as
sources for the quiz programs in [`IDEAS.md`](IDEAS.md), and for the Icelandic terms see
[`GLOSSARY.md`](GLOSSARY.md). Reviewed for objectivity on 2026-10-03.

| Article | Author | Published | Why it is good |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Hvað er marxismi?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=26357) | Nanna Hlín Halldórsdóttir, PhD in philosophy | 2.12.2020 | The newest and broadest. It describes Marxism on its own terms ("gagnrýnið á kapítalisma og vilja annars konar hagkerfi") and calls Rosa Luxemburg "einn áhrifamesti kenningasmiður marxismans". It covers Gramsci, the Frankfurt School and Marxist feminism, and says plainly that it is disputed how much Stalinism has in common with Marxism. Its sources include Jóhann Páll Árnason and *Af marxisma* (Nýhil). |
| [Getið þið sagt mér eitthvað um rússnesku byltinguna 1917 og hvernig hún byrjaði?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=12700) | Sverrir Jakobsson, professor of history, University of Iceland | 11.9.2008 | Good history writing. It explains the causes (the Tsar's oppression, the aftermath of serfdom), credits the women textile workers' strike of 8 March with starting the revolution, and says the October revolution happened with little bloodshed. It lists the reforms (land to the peasants, the eight-hour day) and names the foreign invasion by Britain, France and the US. Its sources are serious historians (E.H. Carr, Arno Mayer, Edward Acton). |
| [Hvað er firring (sem Karl Marx kallaði svo) og finnst hún í samfélaginu í dag?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=2334) | Ottó Másson, philosopher | 24.4.2002 | An in-depth philosophical explanation of Marx's theory of alienation on its own premises, with no polemic. |
| [Er stéttaskipting á Íslandi?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=77352) | Guðmundur Ævar Oddsson, associate professor, University of Akureyri | 29.6.2021 | Academic class analysis built on Weber, Marx and Erik Olin Wright, with Icelandic data. It notes that inequality grew "á uppgangstíma nýfrjálshyggjunnar". |
| [Hvað er verkfall og hver er saga verkfallsréttar í heiminum?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=25341) | Árni Daníel Júlíusson, historian | 31.3.2014 | The history of the strike, from Spartacus to the Flint sit-down strike. It calls the strike "eitt beittasta vopn lágstéttarinnar". |
| [Hver var Karl Marx og hverjir höfðu mest áhrif á hugmyndir hans um samfélagið?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=728) | Ragnheiður Kristjánsdóttir, professor of history, University of Iceland | 4.8.2000 | A short, neutral biography. |
| [Hver var Friedrich Engels og hverjir höfðu mest áhrif á hann?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=729) | Ragnheiður Kristjánsdóttir | 4.8.2000 | A short, neutral biography. |
| [Hver er munurinn á sósíalisma og kommúnisma?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=730) | Ragnheiður Kristjánsdóttir | 4.8.2000 | Neutral and accurate on Marx's critique of the Gotha Programme and Lenin's *State and Revolution*. One simplification: it defines a communist society as one "þar sem einkaeignarétturinn er bannaður", whereas Marx speaks of abolishing private ownership of the means of production. |

## Not listed

[Hver var Vladimir Lenín?](https://www.visindavefur.is/svar.php?id=5029) (2005) uses loaded
Cold War wording, rests on a single source (Encyclopædia Britannica) and dates the Treaty of
Brest-Litovsk to 1917; it was signed in March 1918. For Lenin, use Sverrir Jakobsson's article
on the revolution above.

A good habit from this: a source with an authoritative name can still be wrong. Check a fact
against a second source before you put it in a program.
